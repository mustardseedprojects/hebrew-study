Hebrew Dictionary

Welcome to your Hebrew Dictionary! The purpose of learning Biblical Hebrew is not simply to recognize unfamiliar letters or memorize new words. It is to deepen our understanding of God's Word and discover how the Scriptures reveal His promises, His purposes, and His relationship with His people.

His Word. His Covenant. His Kingdom.

As we learn the language of the Old Testament, we begin to recognize words, expressions, and connections that help us better understand these themes throughout Scripture. This dictionary is a companion to that journey.

Our Scripture Sources

Hebrew Scripture

Our Hebrew text follows the Masoretic textual tradition of the Old Testament, preserving the Hebrew letters and vowel markings that guide its reading.

English Scripture

We use the King James Version (KJV) as our English Scripture text. All English Scripture passages are presented as written in the KJV, without alteration.

The dictionary provides individual Hebrew word meanings to help students understand the language of the original Scriptures. These explanations supplement the KJV; they do not replace or revise its translation.

How to Use This Dictionary

1. Find a Word

Use the main search for an English meaning (such as God), a Hebrew word (such as אֱלֹהִים), or a Scripture reference (such as Genesis 1:1). Use the separate transliteration search for Latin-letter spellings such as Raqia or Rakia. You can also select any Hebrew alphabet letter to browse words beginning with that letter. Select All to see the entire dictionary. Search and alphabet filters work together. Searching Hebrew can reveal related written forms.

2. Discover Its Meaning

Each entry provides the Hebrew word with vowel markings, its transliteration, and its English meaning. Context helps us understand which meaning applies.

3. Listen to the Hebrew

Where verified audio is available, select the audio button to hear the word spoken. Audio is being prepared and checked before release.

4. Discover Where It First Appears

Each entry identifies the first occurrence of that exact Hebrew word form in the Old Testament.

5. Recognize Word Parts

Hebrew often attaches letters to words. For example, הַ (ha) often means the, and וְ (ve) commonly means and.

6. Continue Learning

Return to the Scripture passage where a word appears and explore related forms.

Read the entire verse before examining its individual words. The surrounding passage often helps explain what a word means.
